\section*{Research questions and conservative answers}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Can a sequentially admissible, fully funded strategy
  universally dominate the same-deposit DCA comparator?} No economically
  distinct strategy can: universal pathwise dominance forces the strategy to
  purchase exactly as DCA.
  \item \textbf{Can an economically distinct adaptive strategy retain a
  transparent, model-free relative-wealth floor?} Yes in the frictionless
  comparison model: the epsilon-DCA unit guardrail gives the sharp causal floor
  and leaves a funded discretionary allocation when epsilon is positive.
  \item \textbf{Does the guarded corrected-mean score add empirical value
  inside that safety architecture?} Not in the frozen historical evaluation:
  the floor is confirmed operationally, but incremental value from the
  corrected-mean signal is not confirmed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{DCA, Adaptive Accumulation, and Causal Safety}

\subsection{Which dollar-cost-averaging problem is being studied?}
\label{sec:lit-dca-scope}
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) fixes a nominal purchase amount at regular dates,
but that mechanical description covers economically different questions.
Classical analyses commonly ask how an investor should stage a sum that is
already available. Pye studies irreversible conversion of a given initial sum
under minimax regret, while Constantinides compares DCA with sequential and
nonsequential policies in a portfolio-choice setting
\citep{pye1971,constantinides1979}. Later work evaluates DCA under specified
return dynamics or preferences: Brennan, Li, and Torous study whether mean
reversion can give the heuristic value, and Vanduffel and coauthors construct
an option-based alternative under a L\'evy return model
\citep{brennan2005,vanduffel2012}. Kirkby, Mitra, and Nguyen likewise derive
moments and risk measures in a stochastic DCA and market-timing framework
\citep{kirkby2020}. These are expected or distributional claims under declared
models, not universal terminal-wealth statements.

The present comparison instead models recurring investment: each deposit
becomes available only on its purchase date, and DCA invests that deposit then.
Consequently, the thesis does not answer the lump-sum timing question of whether
cash available at the outset should enter the market immediately or in stages.
That distinction is also explicit in Vanguard's institutional comparison of
immediate and staged investment of an available windfall \citep{finlayzorn2023}.
It also does not study tactical market timing, portfolio rebalancing, or
retrospectively budget-matched strategies whose funding is chosen after the
price path is known. These distinctions are part of the estimand rather than
terminological qualifications. The analysis is research and does not provide
individualized investment advice.

\subsection{Adaptive accumulation changes more than the purchase formula}
\label{sec:lit-adaptive-accumulation}
Several established methods make purchase amounts respond to state. Pye's
sequential minimax rule conditions irreversible conversion on the current
price and the running maximum, but it starts from a fixed initial budget and
optimizes regret within a bounded random-walk problem \citep{pye1971}.
Edleson's value-averaging method instead specifies a portfolio-value path and
adjusts the next contribution---or withdrawal---to close the gap to that path
\citep{edleson2006}. The SmartDCA source varies spending with a reference-to-
current-price ratio and evaluates average acquisition cost
\citep{calvet2023smartdca}. Its funding requirement, reference-price timing,
and unequal-spending comparison therefore have to be supplied before its
cost-per-unit inequalities can be interpreted as an investable wealth
comparison.

Other adaptive proposals change the next contribution after a realized return
or with prevailing macroeconomic conditions and report simulation, historical,
Sharpe-ratio, or stochastic-dominance comparisons
\citep{dunhamfriesen2012,kapalczynskilien2021}. These realized or distributional
findings do not establish a same-deposit guarantee on every positive path.

Calling all three methods ``adaptive DCA'' would hide the relevant differences.
They use different information timing, start from different funding processes,
compare against different policies, optimize different performance criteria,
and offer different guarantee types. In this thesis an adaptive rule must use
only information available when each decision is made, may spend only deposited
cash, and is judged against DCA receiving the same deposits. Adaptivity names
the discretionary purchase response; it does not itself imply forecasting
skill, optimality, or outperformance.

\subsection{Online portfolio guarantees use different trading technologies}
\label{sec:lit-online-decisions}
Online portfolio research provides a mature vocabulary for decisions made
without future information. Cover's universal portfolio is pathwise
competitive, in an asymptotic growth sense, with the best constant-rebalanced
portfolio selected in hindsight; Ordentlich and Cover also characterize the
finite-horizon worst-case wealth ratio to that comparator. Multiplicative-update
algorithms similarly control cumulative log-wealth regret
\citep{cover1991,ordentlichcover1998,helmbold1998}. The online-portfolio literature consequently
centres on repeated allocation of a compounding wealth account and commonly
permits sales needed for rebalancing, as the field survey makes explicit
\citep{lihoi2014}.

One-way trading is closer to buy-only accumulation because conversions are
irreversible, but its competitive guarantees compare a fixed initial budget
with an offline best conversion under stated price information
\citep{elyaniv2001}. None of these guarantees transfers merely by calling a
recurring-purchase rule ``online'': the information set, funding, comparator,
trading permissions, horizon, and payoff must match. A universal pathwise
ratio, an expected-utility comparison, a probabilistic shortfall statement,
and a realized backtest gap are four different claim types.

\subsection{Safety is defined relative to a specified floor}
\label{sec:lit-safety-objectives}
Prior safety frameworks protect different objects. Roy's safety-first criterion
minimizes a bound on the probability of falling below a disaster level
\citep{roy1952}. Constant-proportion portfolio insurance links risky exposure
to wealth above a nonstochastic floor, while drawdown-constrained portfolio
choice keeps wealth above a fraction of its own running maximum
\citep{blackperold1992,grossmanzhou1993}. These are respectively probabilistic,
absolute-floor, and own-history safety concepts, each with its own market and
trading assumptions; jumps can create gap risk for the portfolio-insurance
floor \citep{conttankov2009}.

Conservative online learning supplies a further baseline-relative analogy, but
its safety constraints are expected or high-probability cumulative-reward
statements rather than financial terminal-wealth inequalities \citep{wu2016}.

Epsilon-DCA safety protects a different benchmark: on every admissible positive
price path, cash-inclusive terminal wealth must be at least a fixed fraction of
the same-deposit DCA comparator. It is a universal relative-wealth floor in the
project's frictionless, long-only, buy-only model; it is not an absolute capital
guarantee, a drawdown bound, or evidence of superior expected return. The
guardrail supplies that floor, whereas the corrected-mean score only allocates
the funded discretionary interval.

\subsection{The thesis's comparison boundary}
\label{sec:lit-project-boundary}
Three controls make the comparison economically interpretable. First,
\emph{sequential admissibility} prevents a rule from using a future price to
justify a current purchase. Second, the same-deposit comparator cancels common
external cash flows rather than rewarding a strategy for spending more or for
receiving money earlier. Third, cash-inclusive terminal wealth values both the
asset units and the cash deliberately left uninvested; average acquisition cost
alone cannot do that.

After equal deposits are cancelled, a universal strict improvement would form
a zero-initial-cost, predictable difference strategy, placing the core
impossibility inside the pointwise no-arbitrage envelope of Burzoni and
coauthors \citep{burzoni2019}. The DCA-specific accounting and the
transaction-level equality case remain project results, not claims that the
general no-arbitrage principle is new. This positioning also fixes how later
evidence is read: the theorem may support a universal claim within its model,
whereas deterministic examples and historical estimates report realized or
probabilistic evidence only within their declared designs.
