\section{DCA, Adaptive Accumulation, and Causal Safety}

\subsection{Which dollar-cost-averaging problem is being studied?}
\label{sec:lit-dca-scope}
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) fixes a nominal purchase amount at regular dates,
but that mechanical description covers economically different questions.
Classical analyses commonly ask how an investor should stage a sum that is
already available. Pye studies irreversible conversion of a given initial sum
under minimax regret, while Constantinides compares DCA with sequential and
nonsequential policies in a portfolio-choice setting
\citep{pye1971,constantinides1979}. Later work evaluates DCA under specified
return dynamics or preferences: Brennan, Li, and Torous study whether mean
reversion can give the heuristic value, and Vanduffel and coauthors construct
an option-based alternative under a L\'evy return model
\citep{brennan2005,vanduffel2012}. Kirkby, Mitra, and Nguyen likewise derive
moments and risk measures in a stochastic DCA and market-timing framework
\citep{kirkby2020}. These are expected or distributional claims under declared
models, not universal terminal-wealth statements.

The present comparison instead models recurring investment: each deposit
becomes available only on its purchase date, and DCA invests that deposit then.
Consequently, the thesis does not answer the lump-sum timing question of whether
cash available at the outset should enter the market immediately or in stages.
That distinction is also explicit in Vanguard's institutional comparison of
immediate and staged investment of an available windfall \citep{finlayzorn2023}.
It also does not study tactical market timing, portfolio rebalancing, or
retrospectively budget-matched strategies whose funding is chosen after the
price path is known. These distinctions are part of the estimand rather than
terminological qualifications. The analysis is research and does not provide
individualized investment advice.

\subsection{Adaptive accumulation changes more than the purchase formula}
\label{sec:lit-adaptive-accumulation}
Several established methods make purchase amounts respond to state. Pye's
sequential minimax rule conditions irreversible conversion on the current
price and the running maximum, but it starts from a fixed initial budget and
optimizes regret within a bounded random-walk problem \citep{pye1971}.
Edleson's value-averaging method instead specifies a portfolio-value path and
adjusts the next contribution---or withdrawal---to close the gap to that path
\citep{edleson2006}. The SmartDCA source varies spending with a
reference-to-current-price ratio and evaluates average acquisition cost
\citep{calvet2023smartdca}. Its funding requirement, reference-price timing,
and unequal-spending comparison therefore have to be supplied before its
cost-per-unit inequalities can be interpreted as an investable wealth
comparison.

Other adaptive proposals change the next contribution after a realized return
or with prevailing macroeconomic conditions and report simulation, historical,
Sharpe-ratio, or stochastic-dominance comparisons
\citep{dunhamfriesen2012,kapalczynskilien2021}. These realized or distributional
findings do not establish a same-deposit guarantee on every positive path.

Calling all three methods ``adaptive DCA'' would hide the relevant differences.
They use different information timing, start from different funding processes,
compare against different policies, optimize different performance criteria,
and offer different guarantee types. In this thesis an adaptive rule must use
only information available when each decision is made, may spend only deposited
cash, and is judged against DCA receiving the same deposits. Adaptivity names
the discretionary purchase response; it does not itself imply forecasting
skill, optimality, or outperformance.

\subsection{Online portfolio guarantees use different trading technologies}
\label{sec:lit-online-decisions}
Online portfolio research provides a mature vocabulary for decisions made
without future information. Cover's universal portfolio is pathwise
competitive, in an asymptotic growth sense, with the best constant-rebalanced
portfolio selected in hindsight; Ordentlich and Cover also characterize the
finite-horizon worst-case wealth ratio to that comparator. Multiplicative-update
algorithms similarly control cumulative log-wealth regret
\citep{cover1991,ordentlichcover1998,helmbold1998}. The online-portfolio literature consequently
centres on repeated allocation of a compounding wealth account and commonly
permits sales needed for rebalancing, as the field survey makes explicit
\citep{lihoi2014}.

One-way trading is closer to buy-only accumulation because conversions are
irreversible, but its competitive guarantees compare a fixed initial budget
with an offline best conversion under stated price information
\citep{elyaniv2001}. None of these guarantees transfers merely by calling a
recurring-purchase rule ``online'': the information set, funding, comparator,
trading permissions, horizon, and payoff must match. A universal pathwise
ratio, an expected-utility comparison, a probabilistic shortfall statement,
and a realized backtest gap are four different claim types.

\subsection{Safety is defined relative to a specified floor}
\label{sec:lit-safety-objectives}
Prior safety frameworks protect different objects. Roy's safety-first criterion
minimizes a bound on the probability of falling below a disaster level
\citep{roy1952}. Constant-proportion portfolio insurance links risky exposure
to wealth above a nonstochastic floor, while drawdown-constrained portfolio
choice keeps wealth above a fraction of its own running maximum
\citep{blackperold1992,grossmanzhou1993}. These are respectively probabilistic,
absolute-floor, and own-history safety concepts, each with its own market and
trading assumptions; jumps can create gap risk for the portfolio-insurance
floor \citep{conttankov2009}.

Conservative online learning supplies a further baseline-relative analogy, but
its safety constraints are expected or high-probability cumulative-reward
statements rather than financial terminal-wealth inequalities \citep{wu2016}.

Epsilon-DCA safety protects a different benchmark: on every admissible positive
price path, cash-inclusive terminal wealth must be at least a fixed fraction of
the same-deposit DCA comparator. It is a universal relative-wealth floor in the
project's frictionless, long-only, buy-only model; it is not an absolute capital
guarantee, a drawdown bound, or evidence of superior expected return. The
guardrail supplies that floor, whereas the corrected-mean score only allocates
the funded discretionary interval.

\subsection{The thesis's comparison boundary}
\label{sec:lit-project-boundary}
Three controls make the comparison economically interpretable. First,
\emph{sequential admissibility} prevents a rule from using a future price to
justify a current purchase. Second, the same-deposit comparator cancels common
external cash flows rather than rewarding a strategy for spending more or for
receiving money earlier. Third, cash-inclusive terminal wealth values both the
asset units and the cash deliberately left uninvested; average acquisition cost
alone cannot do that.

After equal deposits are cancelled, a universal strict improvement would form
a zero-initial-cost, predictable difference strategy, placing the core
impossibility inside the pointwise no-arbitrage envelope of Burzoni and
coauthors \citep{burzoni2019}. The DCA-specific accounting and the
transaction-level equality case remain project results, not claims that the
general no-arbitrage principle is new. This positioning also fixes how later
evidence is read: the theorem may support a universal claim within its model,
whereas deterministic examples and historical estimates report realized or
probabilistic evidence only within their declared designs.

\section{Generalized Means and the Corrected Construction}

\subsection{The source object remains a functional until verified}
\label{sec:lit-mean-source-functional}
Appendix B of the SmartDCA source introduces its out quasi-Gini expression as a
``mean'' and records two algebraic reductions, but supplies no reflexivity,
internality, continuity, homogeneity, coordinatewise-monotonicity, or diagonal
argument \citep{calvet2023smartdca}. The thesis therefore calls the unverified
object the \emph{out quasi-Gini functional}. For finite positive inputs and a
positive finite transform $f$, write $\Delta=\alpha-\beta\ne0$; its
constant-input value is

\[
  Q_{\alpha,\beta}^{f}(c,\ldots,c)
  =c^{1/\Delta}f(c)^{(\Delta-1)/\Delta}.
\]

The project classification follows immediately at the necessary reflexivity
seam and is exact: the source functional is a mean precisely when $\Delta=1$ or
$f$ is the identity, and it has a finite extension over the whole diagonal
only in the identity case. Calling this a classification, rather than a proof
of a result asserted by the source, keeps the attribution explicit.

\subsection{Gini, Lehmer, and function-weighted roots}
\label{sec:lit-mean-generalized-roots}
The relevant families are nested specializations, not interchangeable names.
Gini's two-parameter power-sum quotient supplies the classical $G_{p,s}$
family \citep{gini1938}. The Lehmer family is the adjacent-parameter slice,
usually written as a power-weighted arithmetic quotient
\citep{lehmer1971,chuzhao2015}. Beckenbach's function-weighted mean-value functions are
the broader arithmetic-generator predecessor \citep{beckenbach1950}; modern
work uses \emph{Beckenbach--Gini--Lehmer} for this function-weighted
arithmetic form and studies its mean mappings and homogeneity
\citep{matkowskiwrobel2020}. Thus ``generalized mean'' is only an umbrella
description here. It cannot identify the corrected construction or transfer
properties from one subfamily to another.

\subsection{Exact weighted Bajraktarevi\'c identification}
\label{sec:lit-mean-family-identification}
For finite positive inputs $u_i$, positive external weights $w_i$, and a
positive finite transform $f$, the numerator-preserving correction is, off the
diagonal,

\[
 \widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(u;w)
 =\left(
 \frac{\sum_i w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\alpha-1}}
      {\sum_i w_i u_i^{1-\Delta}f(u_i)^{\alpha-1}}
 \right)^{1/\Delta}.
\]

Set $\varphi_\Delta(t)=t^{-\Delta}$ and
$R_\alpha(t)=t f(t)^{\alpha-1}$.
Substitution into the established weighted Bajraktarevi\'c form

\[
 A_{\varphi,R}(u;w)=\varphi^{-1}\!\left(
   \frac{\sum_i w_iR(u_i)\varphi(u_i)}{\sum_i w_iR(u_i)}
 \right)
\]

gives $\widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}
=A_{\varphi_\Delta,R_\alpha}$ exactly. The original function-weighted class and
its modern weighted formulation are prior theory
\citep{bajraktarevic1958,paleszakaria2020}. Because $\varphi_\Delta$ is continuous
and strictly monotone and $R_\alpha$ is positive, established meanhood applies;
it is not supplied by a new general construction in this thesis.

The identification also fixes the special cases. If $f(t)=t^r$, the result is
the classical weighted Gini mean $G_{p,s}$ with
$p=1+r(\alpha-1)$ and $s=p-\Delta$; $f=\mathrm{id}$ gives
$(p,s)=(\alpha,\beta)$. If $\Delta=1$, it is the weighted
Beckenbach--Gini--Lehmer form, and a power function weight gives a weighted
Lehmer mean. On $\Delta=0$, writing $\tau$ for the common parameter, the
adopted parameter-continuous value is the function-weighted geometric
Bajraktarevi\'c mean $A_{\log,R_\tau}$. Modern
weighted treatments record the corresponding Gini branches and comparison
results \citep{lehmer1971,chuzhao2015,matkowskiwrobel2020,
palespasteczka2018,palespasteczka2024}.

\subsection{Property statements stay on their proved loci}
\label{sec:lit-mean-property-boundaries}
Meanhood is not a license to import every familiar property. Continuity in
the inputs is claimed when $f$ is continuous; positivity or monotonicity of
$f$ alone does not exclude a jump. Coordinatewise monotonicity is likewise
not automatic: even the classical weighted Gini family has it exactly on the
region $ps\leq0$, and that criterion does not transfer to an arbitrary
function weight \citep{palespasteczka2024}.

Homogeneity has a sharper boundary. Prior work classifies continuous
homogeneous Bajraktarevi\'c mappings as Gini mappings
\citep{aczeldaroczy1963,palespasteczka2016}. The project's fixed-weight,
two-effective-coordinate characterization specializes that theory: off the
diagonal the corrected mean is degree-one homogeneous exactly when
$\alpha=1$ or $f/f(1)$ is multiplicative; on the diagonal the corresponding
exception is $\tau=1$. Under the project's increasing-transform assumption,
multiplicativity forces $f(t)=Ct^r$. Consequently, a non-power transform is
homogeneous only on the transform-independent $\alpha=1$ slice, including the
diagonal point $(1,1)$, while a power transform makes the whole family a
reparameterized classical weighted Gini family. Neither locus supports a
claim of simultaneous scale invariance and transform novelty.

\subsection{Prior theory and project contributions are kept separate}
\label{sec:lit-mean-contribution-boundary}
The contribution accounting used throughout the thesis is:

\begin{description}
  \item[Prior theory.] Gini, Lehmer, Beckenbach--Gini--Lehmer, and
  Bajraktarevi\'c supply the known families, meanhood framework, and broad
  comparison and homogeneity results.
  \item[Correction.] The project replaces the source denominator by the
  numerator-preserving common-weight normalization and derives its finite
  diagonal branch.
  \item[Classification.] The project proves exactly when the unmodified source
  functional is a mean and when it admits a global finite diagonal extension.
  \item[Characterization.] The project gives the exact transform condition for
  homogeneity of this externally weighted, transform-coupled specialization.
\end{description}

The external boundaries in this accounting are the source text, the weighted
family definition, and the prior homogeneous-mapping result
\citep{calvet2023smartdca,paleszakaria2020,aczeldaroczy1963}.
The corrected construction is not a new general mean class. Its defensible
role is a conservatively identified weighted Bajraktarevi\'c mean used as a
signal after a source correction. The bounded primary-source search supporting
this position does not turn absence of an exact earlier coupling into a
priority claim.

\section{Computational Reproducibility and Statistical Controls}

\subsection{Outcome-blind registration separates evidence tiers}
\label{sec:lit-method-registration}
Repeated evaluation of financial rules against the same history creates a
data-snooping problem: the reported winner can reflect the search rather than
stable predictive content. White's reality-check framework makes the searched
model universe part of the inferential problem rather than evaluating only the
selected rule \citep{white2000datasnooping}. Preregistration addresses a
different but complementary risk. By recording hypotheses and analysis choices
before their outcomes are known, it permits a reader to distinguish tests of
advance claims from analyses suggested by the results
\citep{nosek2018preregistration}. Neither practice certifies that the model is
well specified or that a financial effect is causal.

For this thesis, an outcome-blind protocol fixed H1 and H2, their estimand,
primary grid, circular moving-block bootstrap, and 36-test Holm family before
confirmatory outcome access. The corresponding historical results are the
registered inference. Secondary mechanism summaries are labelled secondary;
the declared but post-confirmatory robustness execution is descriptive
robustness; and any outcome-suggested diagnostic remains exploratory. These
labels preserve the time at which each claim became testable. They do not turn
the repository protocol into model validation. This repository-frozen
outcome-blind registration is not a preregistration deposited in a third-party
registry, and it is not a peer-reviewed Registered Report
\citep{nosek2018preregistration}. Robustness results do not revise the primary
family or rewrite H1 and H2.

\subsection{Overlapping episodes require dependence-aware uncertainty}
\label{sec:lit-method-overlap-resampling}
Rolling investment episodes reuse many of the same market observations. Their
gaps are therefore not independent merely because each nominal start has a
different date. Financial tests based on overlapping observations must account
for the dependence induced by that construction
\citep{richardsonsmith1991overlap}. Block resampling was developed for ordered
stationary observations precisely to retain local dependence that an
independent-observation bootstrap would break; circular block resampling removes
the arbitrary boundary between the first and last candidate block starts
\citep{kunsch1989blockbootstrap,politisromano1992circular}.

The registered procedure works within one asset--horizon cell. It sorts the
episode estimands by ordered monthly episode start, draws fixed-length blocks
with circular wrapping, truncates to the original cell size, and recomputes the
median relative terminal-wealth gap. Block length equals the episode horizon in
rolling-stride units. This circular moving-block bootstrap supplies the
project's dependence-aware uncertainty; the horizon-length choice is a frozen
design choice, not a literature-backed claim of generally optimal block
length or exact finite-sample coverage, especially in sparse long-horizon
cells. Episodes remain overlapping episodes, and the method's interpretation
depends on the declared time-series resampling conditions.

Most importantly, changing the uncertainty calculation does not change the
study design or treatment assignment. Causal identification requires its own
identifying assumptions and design; resampling an observational historical
sequence does not create causal identification \citep{hernanrobins2020}. The
result is uncertainty for realized historical association under the registered
procedure, not a universal, causal, or optimality claim.

\subsection{Multiplicity decisions do not replace effect-size reporting}
\label{sec:lit-method-multiplicity-reporting}
The confirmatory family contains 36 cell-level H1/H2 tests. Holm's sequentially
rejective procedure orders their unadjusted $p$-values and, conditional on
their validity, controls the family-wise error rate through step-down thresholds
\citep{holm1979}. This is material in empirical finance, where testing many
candidate effects against one historical record can produce an inflated
discovery set \citep{harveyliuzhu2016}. Holm adjustment does not repair an
invalid cellwise resampling model, alter a point estimate, or make the tests
independent.

The registered inferential unit must therefore stay visible. Within a declared
asset--horizon cell, $N$ counts ordered rolling episode starts and consecutive
blocks of those starts are resampled; the median relative terminal-wealth gap
is the cell estimand. The 36 hypothesis tests form the multiplicity family, but
counts of cells, assets, horizons, or cross-layer signs are not a second sample
that may be pooled into a new test.

A $p$-value does not measure the magnitude or economic importance of an effect
\citep{wassersteinlazar2016}. The thesis consequently reports signed relative
median gaps as effect sizes, percentile intervals as uncertainty summaries,
unadjusted and Holm-adjusted $p$-values as inferential diagnostics, and the
number of episodes in each cell; interval reporting keeps estimation visible
beside testing \citep{gardneraltman1986}. Robustness rows retain signed effect
summaries within their own schedule and grid, but no unregistered interval or
multiplicity decision is inferred from them.

\subsection{Regeneration, reconciliation, and replication are different}
\label{sec:lit-method-computational-reproducibility}
Computational research is reviewable only when the calculation is linked to
the code and inputs that produced it \citep{peng2011reproducible}. The National
Academies use \emph{reproducibility} for obtaining consistent computational
results from the same data, code, methods, and conditions, while
\emph{replicability} concerns a new study obtaining its own data
\citep{nasem2019reproducibility}. That vocabulary prevents one successful
rerun from being described as independent empirical confirmation.

The release therefore makes two narrower claims. \emph{Deterministic
regeneration} reruns accepted software on the same accepted inputs and compares
the resulting files or declared values with the retained bundle.
\emph{Independent reconciliation} uses a separately implemented route to
recompute selected episode joins, aggregates, bootstrap streams, Holm values,
and report summaries against the retained evidence. Reconciliation can expose
a shared-runner or transcription defect, but because it uses the same provider
observations it is not independent-data replication. A public reproduction
route can regenerate the unrestricted deterministic and synthesis layers;
access-controlled historical checks retain a more limited, receipt-bound
claim.

\subsection{Provenance binds artifacts without validating finance claims}
\label{sec:lit-method-provenance-release}
Provenance records relate entities, generating activities, and responsible
agents; they describe how an artifact came to exist rather than whether its
scientific interpretation is true \citep{moreaumissier2013prov}. Hash-based
names can bind an identifier to digital content
\citep{farrell2013rfc6920}. The repository applies those ideas through
versioned protocols and inputs, content-derived run identities, manifests, and
SHA-256 fingerprints. The binding is only as authoritative as the trusted
starting identifier and does not validate source authority. Here
\emph{immutable} means that an accepted identity is not overwritten and changed
bytes require a new identity. A digest makes that change detectable under the
named algorithm; it does not make storage physically immutable.

A provider-data receipt records source semantics, provenance metadata, a
fingerprint, schema, coverage, parser identity, and the release decision. It is
evidence about retained bytes, not a substitute for those bytes. FAIR's access
principles explicitly permit authentication and authorization where necessary
and separately call for a clear usage licence
\citep{wilkinson2016fair}. Reproducible identification and authorized private
rechecking therefore do not by themselves grant public redistribution.

These controls make the computation auditable: public artifacts can be
regenerated, while an authorized reviewer can reconcile restricted provider
observations against sanitized receipts and derived outputs. Software
engineering and provenance evidence does not establish a financial result;
financial evidence still comes from the model, proofs, empirical design, and
observed estimates under their own assumptions.

\chapter{Financial Model and Corrected Signal Foundations}
\label{ch:model-signal}

The investment problem comes first. A recurring-investment algorithm observes
cash deposits and market prices in sequence, decides how much deposited cash to
convert into an asset, and carries the remainder forward. Comparing such an
algorithm with DCA is meaningful only after its information, funding, and
terminal accounting have been fixed. The financial comparison is fixed before
the specialized mean theory is used. The mean enters later as one possible
reference-price computation; it neither defines the budget nor determines the
performance criterion.

\section{Recurring Investment and the Comparison Model}
\label{sec:comparison-model}

Fix a finite horizon of $n\geq1$ purchase dates indexed by
$t=1,\ldots,n$. At date $t$, the asset has an observed finite positive price
$p_t>0$ and a nonnegative exogenous cash deposit $d_t\geq0$ becomes
available. After the last purchase, every strategy is valued at the same
finite positive evaluation price $P>0$. This manuscript writes $P$ for the
quantity indexed as $p_{n+1}$ in the canonical impossibility theorem. The
price and deposit sequences are otherwise unrestricted: no return law,
forecast, or favorable path shape is built into the comparison.

A strategy $S$ is \emph{sequentially admissible} when its date-$t$ purchase
uses only prices, deposits, and portfolio state observed through date $t$.
The decision occurs after observing $(p_t,d_t)$ and before any future price is
known. Let $b_t^S$ denote its cash outlay on the asset at that date; this is
the manuscript symbol for the repository's $x_t$. Let $C_t^S$ and $Q_t^S$
denote, respectively, cash carried and asset units held immediately after the
purchase. Starting from $C_0^S=Q_0^S=0$, long-only, buy-only, fully funded
trading with no leverage is exactly

\begin{equation}
  0\leq b_t^S\leq C_{t-1}^S+d_t,\qquad
  C_t^S=C_{t-1}^S+d_t-b_t^S,\qquad
  Q_t^S=Q_{t-1}^S+\frac{b_t^S}{p_t}.
  \label{eq:model-recursions}
\end{equation}

Unused cash carries without interest. Purchases cannot be negative, existing
units cannot be sold, and the currently available cash is the complete budget.
In particular, knowing that a lower price will occur later would be an oracle
assumption rather than sequential admissibility. These restrictions describe
the Financial Computing problem addressed here: construct a causal allocation
rule inside an auditable cash ledger, then evaluate it under an economically
fair comparator.

\section{Exact Accounting and the DCA Comparator}

\subsection{Portfolio recursion and full funding}
\label{sec:portfolio-recursion}

Iterating (\ref{eq:model-recursions}) yields two identities that every
admissible strategy must satisfy:

\begin{equation}
  C_t^S=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(d_i-b_i^S)\geq0,
  \qquad
  Q_t^S=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{b_i^S}{p_i}.
  \label{eq:model-ledger}
\end{equation}

The first identity is the prefix funding constraint. It does not require
$b_t^S\leq d_t$ date by date: cash retained earlier may finance a later
purchase larger than the current deposit. It does require every prefix of the
cash ledger to remain nonnegative. The second identity records units rather
than money, so purchases made at different prices remain comparable.

\subsection{Same-deposit DCA}
\label{sec:comparators}

Use the superscript $D$ for the DCA policy. DCA receives the same deposit
sequence and the same evaluation horizon as the candidate strategy. It spends
each new deposit immediately,

\begin{equation}
  b_t^D=d_t,\qquad C_t^D=0,\qquad
  Q_t^D=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{d_i}{p_i}.
  \label{eq:model-dca}
\end{equation}

Giving DCA different cash flows, making the candidate's total budget depend on
the realized path, or matching spending only after the fact would define a
different comparison. Here the deposit sequence and horizon are common inputs,
so the only difference is how each admissible policy times purchases and
carries cash.

\subsection{Cash-inclusive terminal wealth}
\label{sec:terminal-wealth}

Terminal wealth of policy $S$ at the common evaluation price is

\begin{equation}
  W_n^S(P)=C_n^S+P Q_n^S,
  \qquad
  W_n^D(P)=P Q_n^D.
  \label{eq:model-terminal-wealth}
\end{equation}

Thus cash is not discarded merely because it was not converted into the
asset. Combining (\ref{eq:model-ledger})--(\ref{eq:model-terminal-wealth})
gives the exact realized-path identity

\begin{equation}
  W_n^S(P)-W_n^D(P)
  =\sum_{t=1}^{n}(d_t-b_t^S)
    \left(1-\frac{P}{p_t}\right).
  \label{eq:model-dca-gap}
\end{equation}

Equation (\ref{eq:model-dca-gap}) is accounting, not a performance promise.
It shows why a dollar deliberately held back can help when its purchase-date
price exceeds $P$ and hurt when that price is below $P$. Chapter
\ref{ch:safety} will use the unrestricted path quantifier to turn this
observation into the causal impossibility boundary; at this point it only fixes
what is being compared.

\subsection{Average acquisition cost is not performance}
\label{sec:accounting-quantities}

When $Q_t^S>0$, define average acquisition cost by

\begin{equation}
  AC_t^S=
  \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_i^S}{Q_t^S}
  =\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_i^S}
         {\sum_{i=1}^{t}b_i^S/p_i}.
  \label{eq:model-acquisition-cost}
\end{equation}

It is undefined when no units have been acquired. Where defined, it is the
spending-weighted harmonic purchase price. The source study uses acquisition
cost to compare allocations whose spending can differ
\citep{calvet2023smartdca}. A lower value of $AC_t^S$ can reflect buying fewer
units at low prices while retaining more cash; it does not determine either
$Q_t^S-Q_t^D$ or the cash-inclusive gap in
(\ref{eq:model-terminal-wealth}). Average acquisition cost is therefore an
accounting quantity, not a budget-equivalent performance criterion. All
economic comparisons in this thesis use common deposits and terminal wealth.

\section{Auditing the Source Functional}
\label{sec:source-functional-audit}

Only after the financial model is fixed does the specialized mean question
become relevant. The source proposes a transformed reference price and calls
its two-parameter extension an out quasi-Gini mean
\citep{calvet2023smartdca}. The source assumes that its transform is positive
and increasing. The project's classification is stronger: it needs only
positivity. Accordingly, for a finite positive input vector
$u=(u_1,\ldots,u_m)$, an arbitrary positive finite transform
$f:(0,\infty)\mathbin{\to}(0,\infty)$, and real parameters $\alpha,\beta$
with $\Delta=\alpha-\beta\neq0$, write the unmodified source object as

\begin{equation}
  Q_{\alpha,\beta}^{f}(u)
  =\left(
    \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_i f(u_i)^{\alpha-1}}
         {\sum_{i=1}^{m}f(u_i)^{\beta}}
   \right)^{1/\Delta}.
  \label{eq:source-functional}
\end{equation}

It remains the \emph{out quasi-Gini functional} until its mean properties are
established. A mean must return $c$ on every constant vector. Instead,

\begin{equation}
  Q_{\alpha,\beta}^{f}(c,\ldots,c)
  =c^{1/\Delta}f(c)^{(\Delta-1)/\Delta}.
  \label{eq:source-constant}
\end{equation}

The defect is structural rather than a missing regularity condition: even a
positive, continuous, strictly increasing transform can fail reflexivity.

\subsection{Exact source-functional classification}
\label{thm:source-classification}

For fixed finite arity, positive inputs, arbitrary positive $f$, and
$\Delta\neq0$, the source functional (\ref{eq:source-functional}) is a mean
if and only if

\begin{equation}
  \Delta=1
  \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
  f(u)=u\ \hbox{for every }u>0.
  \label{eq:source-classification}
\end{equation}

Moreover, a finite parameter-continuous extension valid on the entire diagonal
$\alpha=\beta$ exists only for the identity transform. The constant-vector
calculation supplies necessity; on $\Delta=1$ the expression is a positively
weighted arithmetic mean, while $f=\mathrm{id}$ gives the classical Gini mean.
Appendix \ref{sec:proof-source-classification} supplies the complete argument
and diagonal calculation.

The classification preserves the source's two correct compatibility
identities. The identity transform yields the classical Gini quotient, and
$\Delta=1$ yields the source's out quasi-Lehmer form. It does not transfer
continuity, degree-one homogeneity, or coordinatewise monotonicity to a general
positive increasing transform. Those properties require separate hypotheses
or separate characterizations.

\section{The Corrected Out Quasi-Gini Mean}

\subsection{Numerator-preserving normalization}
\label{def:corrected-mean}

Let the mean inputs $u_i>0$ carry fixed external weights $w_i>0$. The adopted
correction retains the source numerator but gives numerator and denominator a
common positive weight structure. Off the diagonal, define

\begin{equation}
  \widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(u;w)
  =\left(
    \frac{\sum_i w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\alpha-1}}
         {\sum_i w_i u_i^{1-\Delta}f(u_i)^{\alpha-1}}
   \right)^{1/\Delta}.
  \label{eq:corrected-mean-off-diagonal}
\end{equation}

On the diagonal, write $\alpha=\beta=\tau$ and define the
parameter-continuous branch

\begin{equation}
  \widehat G_{\tau,\tau}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(u;w)
  =\exp\left(
    \frac{\sum_i w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\tau-1}\log u_i}
         {\sum_i w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\tau-1}}
  \right).
  \label{eq:corrected-mean-diagonal}
\end{equation}

The denominator in (\ref{eq:corrected-mean-off-diagonal}) is obtained by
replacing the source numerator's $u_i$ by $u_i^{1-\Delta}$. Consequently a
constant input $c$ produces a ratio $c^\Delta$ and the output is exactly $c$.
This is the numerator-preserving repair selected by the project; it is not
claimed to be the only imaginable causal normalization.

\subsection{Known family and compatibility identities}
\label{sec:corrected-family-identification}

Set the Bajraktarevi\'c generator and positive weight function to
$\varphi_\Delta(u)=u^{-\Delta}$ and
$R_\alpha(u)=u f(u)^{\alpha-1}$. Direct substitution gives

\begin{equation}
  \widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}
  =A_{\varphi_\Delta,R_\alpha},
  \label{eq:corrected-bajraktarevic}
\end{equation}

exactly in the established weighted Bajraktarevi\'c class
\citep{bajraktarevic1958,paleszakaria2020}. Meanhood follows from this
identification, or directly from the positive weighted-power representation in
Appendix \ref{sec:proof-corrected-mean}. The correction is therefore not a new
general mean class.

Its relevant special cases are exact:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $f=\mathrm{id}$ gives the classical weighted Gini mean
  $G_{\alpha,\beta}$;
  \item $\Delta=1$ gives the weighted Beckenbach--Gini--Lehmer form and
  preserves the source-aligned out quasi-Lehmer slice;
  \item a power transform $f(u)=Cu^r$ gives the classical weighted Gini mean
  $G_{p,s}$ with $p=1+r(\alpha-1)$ and $s=p-\Delta$; and
  \item (\ref{eq:corrected-mean-diagonal}) is the corresponding
  function-weighted geometric Bajraktarevi\'c value.
\end{itemize}

These established family relations and naming boundaries are documented in
the primary mean literature
\citep{matkowskiwrobel2020,palespasteczka2018,palespasteczka2024}. The
project contribution at this seam is the correction and its bounded
classification and characterization, not the underlying family.

\subsection{Homogeneity has an exact boundary}
\label{thm:homogeneity}

With fixed positive external weights and at least two effective coordinates,
the off-diagonal corrected mean is degree-one homogeneous if and only if
$\alpha=1$ or $f/f(1)$ is multiplicative. On the diagonal, $\tau=1$ is the
corresponding transform-independent exception; away from it, the same
normalized-multiplicativity condition is necessary and sufficient. Under any
of monotonicity, measurability, or continuity, this condition forces
$f(u)=Cu^r$ for some $C>0$ and real $r$. The continuous homogeneous
Bajraktarevi\'c mappings are already known to reduce to Gini mappings
\citep{aczeldaroczy1963,palespasteczka2016}; the project's result is the exact
condition on this displayed transform coupling.

There is a practical cost to the characterization. A non-power increasing
transform is homogeneous only on the transform-blind $\alpha=1$ locus, whereas
a power transform makes the whole family a reparameterized classical Gini
family. Scale invariance and transform novelty therefore cannot be obtained
simultaneously from the corrected mean evaluated on raw price levels. The proof
in Appendix \ref{sec:proof-corrected-homogeneity} also records the fixed-weight
and two-effective-coordinate qualifications. No general
coordinatewise-monotonicity conclusion is imported from meanhood.

\section{A Normalized Lagged Corrected-Mean Signal}
\label{sec:corrected-signal}

The homogeneity boundary makes an unnormalized currency-price reference
unsuitable for a general transform. The signal instead anchors every observed
price to the first one. Define the dimensionless price $z_i=p_i/p_1$. For
$t\geq2$, use equal external weights and only the lagged history:

\begin{equation}
  R_{t-1}
  =\widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}
    (z_1,\ldots,z_{t-1};1,\ldots,1),
  \qquad
  r_t=\frac{z_t}{R_{t-1}}.
  \label{eq:corrected-reference}
\end{equation}

The reference $R_{t-1}$ is positive, dimensionless, and unchanged by a common
currency rescaling of every $p_i$. It excludes the current price, so a change
in $p_t$ cannot move its own benchmark. At the first date set $r_1=1$; this is
the only warm-up convention. Equal weights add no recency or horizon
hyperparameter.

Normalize the source-style score at the neutral relative price and map its
odds to a bounded discretionary fraction:

\begin{equation}
  a_t
  =\frac{1}{1+\left(f(r_t)/f(1)\right)^{1-\alpha}},
  \qquad 0<a_t<1.
  \label{eq:corrected-score}
\end{equation}

Equivalently,
$a_t/(1-a_t)=(f(r_t)/f(1))^{\alpha-1}$, so
$\alpha-1=\rho$ retains the source score parameter's meaning
\citep{calvet2023smartdca}. One-point and constant price histories give
$r_t=1$ and $a_t=1/2$. Holding the lagged history fixed, if $f$ is
nondecreasing and $\alpha\leq1$, the score is nonincreasing in the current
price; strict increase of $f$ and $\alpha<1$ make the response strict. No
comparative statics in lagged prices are asserted for a general transform.

The normalized lagged reference supplies a causal signal, not a safety
guarantee. Boundedness of $a_t$ says only that it can select a fraction of some
specified feasible interval. It does not establish DCA dominance, a
relative-wealth floor, or empirical forecasting value. Chapter \ref{ch:safety} will
construct the separately proved unit guardrail, define the funded
discretionary interval, and use $a_t$ only as a selector inside that interval.
This separation keeps the financial guarantee attached to the constraint that
proves it rather than to the corrected mean.

\section{Proof of the Source-Functional Classification}
\label{sec:proof-source-classification}

Fix $m\geq1$, $\Delta=\alpha-\beta\neq0$, and a positive transform $f$.
For the source functional in (\ref{eq:source-functional}), a constant vector
gives (\ref{eq:source-constant}). If the functional is a mean, reflexivity
requires

\begin{equation}
  f(c)^{\Delta-1}=c^{\Delta-1}
  \qquad\hbox{for every }c>0.
  \label{eq:appendix-source-reflexivity}
\end{equation}

When $\Delta\neq1$, positivity and injectivity of a nonzero real power force
$f(c)=c$ for all $c>0$. Hence $\Delta=1$ or $f=\mathrm{id}$ is necessary,
without any continuity or monotonicity hypothesis.

Both alternatives are sufficient. If $\Delta=1$, then

\begin{equation}
  Q_{\beta+1,\beta}^{f}(u)
  =\frac{\sum_i u_i f(u_i)^\beta}{\sum_i f(u_i)^\beta},
  \label{eq:appendix-source-gap-one}
\end{equation}

which is an arithmetic mean with strictly positive, input-dependent weights.
If $f=\mathrm{id}$, then

\begin{equation}
  Q_{\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{id}}(u)
  =\left(\frac{\sum_i u_i^\alpha}{\sum_i u_i^\beta}
   \right)^{1/\Delta}.
  \label{eq:appendix-source-gini}
\end{equation}

Inside the parentheses is a positive weighted average of $u_i^\Delta$ with
weights $u_i^\beta$. For $\Delta>0$, applying the increasing
$1/\Delta$ power preserves the minimum and maximum bounds. For
$\Delta<0$, the power and the ordering of $u_i^\Delta$ both reverse, so the
same internality follows. This proves the if-and-only-if classification.

For completeness, consider the diagonal. Hold $\beta$ fixed and let
$\Delta\to0$. Put

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal N_0=\sum_i u_i f(u_i)^{\beta-1},
  \qquad \mathcal D_0=\sum_i f(u_i)^\beta.
\end{equation}

Then

\begin{equation}
  \log Q_{\beta+\Delta,\beta}^{f}(u)
  =\frac{1}{\Delta}\log\left(
    \frac{\sum_i u_i f(u_i)^{\beta-1+\Delta}}{\mathcal D_0}
  \right).
  \label{eq:appendix-source-diagonal-log}
\end{equation}

Unless $\mathcal N_0/\mathcal D_0=1$, the logarithm has a nonzero constant term and division by
$\Delta$ produces opposite zero/infinite one-sided behavior. A finite limit
for every positive vector must hold in particular at $(c,\ldots,c)$, where
$\mathcal N_0/\mathcal D_0=c/f(c)$; thus it forces $f=\mathrm{id}$. In that identity case,
l'Hopital's rule gives the established Gini diagonal

\begin{equation}
  Q_{\beta,\beta}^{\mathrm{id}}(u)
  =\exp\left(
    \frac{\sum_i u_i^\beta\log u_i}{\sum_i u_i^\beta}
  \right).
  \label{eq:appendix-source-diagonal}
\end{equation}

The obstruction cannot be removed by stronger regularity. For example,
$f(u)=2u$, $(\alpha,\beta)=(2,0)$, and a constant vector of ones give
$Q_{2,0}^{f}=\sqrt{2}$ rather than one, although $f$ is positive,
continuous, and strictly increasing. Separate examples in the canonical audit
also show that continuity, homogeneity, and coordinatewise monotonicity are not
automatic; none is needed for the exact classification proved above.

\section{The Corrected Mean and Its Diagonal Limit}
\label{sec:proof-corrected-mean}

For $\xi\neq0$, write the positive weighted power mean as

\begin{equation}
  P_\xi(u;\omega)
  =\left(\frac{\sum_i\omega_i u_i^\xi}{\sum_i\omega_i}\right)^{1/\xi}.
\end{equation}

At fixed off-diagonal parameters, define the positive effective weights

\begin{equation}
  \omega_i=w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\alpha-1}.
\end{equation}

Equation (\ref{eq:corrected-mean-off-diagonal}) becomes

\begin{equation}
  \widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(u;w)
  =P_{-\Delta}(u;\omega).
  \label{eq:appendix-corrected-power}
\end{equation}

This is a weighted power mean at the realized input with strictly positive
weights. The same order-reversal argument used above shows that it lies
between the smallest and largest $u_i$ for either sign of $\Delta$.
Positivity, symmetry under simultaneous permutation of inputs and weights, and
reflexivity follow directly. This proves meanhood without treating the
input-dependent $\omega_i$ as fixed external weights in comparative
statements.

For the full diagonal claim, let $\Delta\to0$ along any parameter path with
$\alpha(\Delta)\to\tau$ and
$\beta(\Delta)=\alpha(\Delta)-\Delta$. Put
$\omega_i(\Delta)=w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\alpha(\Delta)-1}$ and normalize these
weights by
$\pi_i(\Delta)=\omega_i(\Delta)/\sum_j\omega_j(\Delta)$. Then

\begin{equation}
  \log\widehat G_{\alpha(\Delta),\beta(\Delta)}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(u;w)
  =-\frac{1}{\Delta}\log\left(
    \sum_i\pi_i(\Delta)\exp(-\Delta\log u_i)
  \right).
  \label{eq:appendix-corrected-diagonal-path}
\end{equation}

The finite collection of normalized weights converges to

\begin{equation}
  \pi_i(0)=
  \frac{w_i u_i f(u_i)^{\tau-1}}
       {\sum_j w_j u_j f(u_j)^{\tau-1}}.
\end{equation}

Expanding the finite sum and then its logarithm at $\Delta=0$ gives

\begin{equation}
  \lim_{\Delta\to0}
  \log\widehat G_{\alpha(\Delta),\beta(\Delta)}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(u;w)
  =\sum_i\pi_i(0)\log u_i.
  \label{eq:appendix-corrected-diagonal-limit}
\end{equation}

Exponentiating gives (\ref{eq:corrected-mean-diagonal}). Only the finitely many
positive values $f(u_i)$ enter this calculation; differentiability of $f$ as
a function of its input is unnecessary, and the conclusion does not depend on
the approach direction in the $(\alpha,\beta)$ plane. This cancellation is
precisely what the source denominator lacks.

The compatibility identities also follow algebraically. Setting
$f=\mathrm{id}$ reduces the numerator and denominator exponents to
$\alpha$ and $\beta$. Setting $\Delta=1$ removes $u_i$ from the denominator
and gives the weighted source out quasi-Lehmer expression. Finally,
$f(u)=Cu^r$ cancels the common power of $C$ and produces $G_{p,s}$ with
$p=1+r(\alpha-1)$ and $s=p-\Delta$.

\section{Proof of the Homogeneity Characterization}
\label{sec:proof-corrected-homogeneity}

For a positive auxiliary function $\eta$ and fixed positive external weights,
consider

\begin{equation}
  M_{\Delta,\eta}(u;w)
  =\left(\frac{\sum_i w_i \eta(u_i)u_i^\Delta}
                 {\sum_i w_i \eta(u_i)}\right)^{1/\Delta}
  \quad(\Delta\neq0).
  \label{eq:appendix-weighted-power-form}
\end{equation}

Suppose at least two coordinates carry positive weight. If this mapping is
degree-one homogeneous, compare two unequal inputs $u$ and $v$ before and
after multiplication by $c>0$; for a larger arity, set every remaining input
equal to one of these two so that its positive weights combine with that
group. Cancelling $c^\Delta$ and cross-multiplying the two weighted averages
yields

\begin{equation}
  (u^\Delta-v^\Delta)w_1w_2
  \{\eta(cu)\eta(v)-\eta(u)\eta(cv)\}=0.
  \label{eq:appendix-homogeneity-ratio}
\end{equation}

Because $u\neq v$ and $\Delta\neq0$, the first factor is nonzero. Hence
$\eta(cu)/\eta(u)$ is independent of $u$: write
$\eta(cu)=\kappa(c)\eta(u)$. Setting $u=1$ gives
$\kappa(c)=\eta(c)/\eta(1)$, so $\eta/\eta(1)$ is multiplicative.
Conversely, normalized multiplicativity makes the common scale factor cancel from
(\ref{eq:appendix-weighted-power-form}), proving sufficiency.

The corrected mean has

\begin{equation}
  \eta(u)=u^{1-\Delta}f(u)^{\alpha-1}.
  \label{eq:appendix-corrected-weight}
\end{equation}

The power $u^{1-\Delta}$ is multiplicative. Thus
(\ref{eq:appendix-corrected-weight}) is multiplicative automatically when
$\alpha=1$ and, otherwise, exactly when $f/f(1)$ is multiplicative. This
proves the off-diagonal criterion, including negative $\Delta$.

On the diagonal, replace the power average by

\begin{equation}
  M_{0,\eta}(u;w)
  =\exp\left(
    \frac{\sum_i w_i \eta(u_i)\log u_i}{\sum_i w_i \eta(u_i)}
  \right).
\end{equation}

The same two-point cross-multiplication uses $\log u-\log v$ instead of
$u^\Delta-v^\Delta$. In (\ref{eq:corrected-mean-diagonal}), the auxiliary
function is $\eta(u)=u f(u)^{\tau-1}$, yielding the $\tau=1$ exception and otherwise
the same normalized-multiplicativity condition.

Finally, if $\mu=f/f(1)$ is positive and multiplicative, then
$\mu(u)=\exp(\ell(\log u))$ for an additive function $\ell$ on the real line.
Without regularity, non-linear additive functions exist, so multiplicativity
is the exact statement. Monotonicity, measurability, or continuity makes $\ell$
linear and gives $f(u)=Cu^r$. Substitution then recovers a classical weighted
Gini mean. The necessity proof fails for only one effective coordinate, where
every mean equals that coordinate, and it always holds the external weights
fixed under input rescaling. These are the qualifications stated in Chapter
\ref{ch:model-signal}.
