## Question

Compare the smallest viable normalizations of the source functional and choose a canonical corrected definition that earns the name mean, preserves both required compatibility identities, admits a diagonal extension and positive external weights, and remains useful for a causal SmartDCA construction.

## Answer

Write \(d=\alpha-\beta\ne0\). The source quotient mixes two different
positive weight systems: its numerator is the numerator generated by

\[
h_{1}(t)=f(t)^\beta\left(\frac{f(t)}{t}\right)^{d-1},
\]

whereas its denominator is generated by \(h_0(t)=f(t)^\beta\). The smallest
repairs replace this mismatch by one common weight. More generally, the bridge

\[
h_\lambda(t)
=f(t)^\beta\left(\frac{f(t)}{t}\right)^{\lambda(d-1)},
\qquad
M_{\lambda;\alpha,\beta}^f(x;w)
=\left(
\frac{\sum_i w_i h_\lambda(x_i)x_i^d}
     {\sum_i w_i h_\lambda(x_i)}
\right)^{1/d}
\tag{1}
\]

contains the denominator-preserving repair at \(\lambda=0\), the balanced
repair at \(\lambda=\tfrac12\), and the numerator-preserving repair at
\(\lambda=1\). Thus the two one-sided repairs are equally small algebraically;
meanhood alone does not make the correction unique.

For every real \(\lambda\), positive \(f\), positive external weights \(w_i\),
and \(d\ne0\), (1) is an internal mean in the standard mean-theory sense used
in ticket 01. The quotient is a positive weighted average of the \(x_i^d\);
applying the \(1/d\) power gives internality for both signs of \(d\), and
reflexivity follows. In the weighted setting, symmetry means invariance under
simultaneous permutation of the pairs \((x_i,w_i)\); unequal fixed weights do
not give symmetry under permutation of the inputs alone. Moreover:

- if \(f(t)=t\), then \(h_\lambda(t)=t^\beta\) for every \(\lambda\), so (1)
  is exactly the weighted classical Gini mean \(G_{\alpha,\beta}\);
- if \(d=1\), then \(h_\lambda(t)=f(t)^\beta\) for every \(\lambda\), so (1)
  is exactly the weighted source out quasi-Lehmer mean; and
- as \(\alpha,\beta\to q\) and \(d\to0\), (1) has the path-independent finite
  extension
  \[
  M_{\lambda;q,q}^f(x;w)
  =\exp\!\left(
  \frac{\sum_i w_i x_i^\lambda f(x_i)^{q-\lambda}\log x_i}
       {\sum_i w_i x_i^\lambda f(x_i)^{q-\lambda}}
  \right).
  \tag{2}
  \]

The other map-level properties remain separate from meanhood and can be stated
precisely at this decision point. Input continuity follows if \(f\) is
continuous but not from positivity and increase alone. Degree-one homogeneity is not
automatic (for example, \(f(t)=1+t\) on the \(d=1\) slice), and
coordinatewise monotonicity can fail even for \(f=\mathrm{id}\) and
\((\alpha,\beta)=(2,1)\). For the canonical \(\lambda=1\) choice with
\(\alpha\) fixed,

\[
M_{1;\alpha,\alpha-d}^f
=P_{-d}\bigl(x;\,w_i x_i f(x_i)^{\alpha-1}\bigr),
\]

so it is nonincreasing in \(d\) by power-mean ordering; no general monotonicity
claim is made when \(\alpha\) and the function weights vary together. Equation
(2) settles the finite diagonal limit. Complete sharp property regions and
other parameter limits remain the later axiom ticket's job.

The canonical choice is **the numerator-preserving repair \(\lambda=1\)**:

\[
\boxed{
\widehat G_{\alpha,\beta}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(x;w)
=\left(
\frac{\sum_i w_i x_i f(x_i)^{\alpha-1}}
     {\sum_i w_i x_i^{\,1-\alpha+\beta}f(x_i)^{\alpha-1}}
\right)^{1/(\alpha-\beta)}
}
\tag{3}
\]

off the diagonal, with

\[
\boxed{
\widehat G_{q,q}^{f,\mathrm{out}}(x;w)
=\exp\!\left(
\frac{\sum_i w_i x_i f(x_i)^{q-1}\log x_i}
     {\sum_i w_i x_i f(x_i)^{q-1}}
\right)
}
\tag{4}
\]

on the diagonal. This is exactly the weighted Bajraktarević mean
\(A_{t^{-d},\,t f(t)^{\alpha-1}}\) identified in ticket 03, so the definition
itself is prior theory and must not be presented as a new mean class.

The tie-breaker is conservative parameter semantics, not a uniqueness theorem
about causality. With a causally observed price ratio \(r_t\), the source
out-SmartDCA score is \(f(r_t)^\rho=f(r_t)^{\alpha-1}\). Formula (3) leaves the
complete source numerator \(r_t f(r_t)^{\alpha-1}\), and hence this score and
parameter meaning, unchanged. The \(\lambda=0\) repair instead attaches its
common weight to \(\beta\), while the balanced repair introduces an additional
blend. Positive external weights can carry exogenous per-date scaling without
disturbing meanhood.

All members of (1) can supply scores computed from current information, so
causality alone does not select \(\lambda=1\). Nor does (3) itself provide an
off-slice acquisition-cost identity: spending proportionally to
\(f(r_t)^{\alpha-1}\) produces a cash-spent denominator
\(\sum_t f(r_t)^{\alpha-1}\), which matches (3)'s denominator only on \(d=1\).
The canonical choice therefore remains useful as a corrected statistic and as
a semantics-preserving causal score family, while a later ticket must decide
whether and how to map it into fully funded purchases with exact accounting.
By ticket 04, neither (3) nor that future map may be claimed to universally
dominate DCA on all positive paths unless it collapses to DCA; a positive
comparison must use an explicit restricted path class or performance criterion.

Boundary checks were performed on constant inputs, both compatibility slices,
both signs of \(d\), and nontrivial weighted inputs. For example, with
\(x=(3,3,3)\), \(w=(1,2,1)\), \(f(t)=1+t\), and
\((\alpha,\beta)=(3,1)\), the source gives \(\sqrt{12}\), while every member
of (1) gives \(3\). With \(x=(1,2,4)\) under the same transform, weights, and
parameters, the \(\lambda=0,\tfrac12,1\) values are respectively
\(2.85549403\), \(2.77784126\), and \(2.69679945\), all inside \([1,4]\).
For the canonical repair with \(x=(1,2,5)\), \(w=(1,2,1)\),
\(f(t)=1+t\), and \(q=0.7\), the values at
\(d=-10^{-3}\) and \(d=10^{-3}\) are \(2.75331513\) and \(2.75369666\),
converging to the diagonal value \(2.75350589\) in (4).
